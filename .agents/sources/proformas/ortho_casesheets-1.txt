ORTHO CASE SHEETS — transcribed from the owner's handwritten notes

HOW THIS WAS READ, because it matters for trusting it
-----------------------------------------------------
`ortho_casesheets-1.pdf` is a CamScanner scan: 22 pages of handwriting, with no
text layer at all. `extract-pdf-text.py` returned 506 bytes of the words
"Scanned by CamScanner" repeated 22 times, which is what this file used to hold.

OCR was not available — tesseract, poppler-utils, pypdf and pip are all refused
by the sandbox's egress proxy. So the pages were read directly instead: the PDF
embeds each page as a DCTDecode stream, and a DCTDecode stream IS a JPEG
verbatim, so the 22 page images were written out byte-for-byte with a few lines
of Python and read as images.

That means this is a TRANSCRIPTION BY EYE, not machine OCR. It is faithful to
the structure and the clinical content, but it is not character-exact, and the
patient names, ages and localities in the original worked examples are left out
deliberately — a proforma is the empty form, not one filled copy of it.

**Where this disagrees with the owner's own sheet, the sheet wins.**

1. CTEV
=======
Chief complaint: deformity in (R) leg since birth. Child brought by mother,
whose reliability is recorded.

HOPI: deformity identified at birth. After 15 days, POP (below-knee cast)
applied to both legs for 3 months. After 3 months the (L) side was corrected;
only little improvement on the (R) leg. Then advised CTEV splint. Now referred.

Past H/o: non-consanguineous marriage.
Antenatal H/o: no oligohydramnios, no TORCH infections, no multiple pregnancy.
Natal H/o: FTNVD, 1st child.
Developmental H/o: attained as per age.
Immunisation H/o: immunised up to age.
Family H/o: no significant family history.

General examination: alert and awake. No PICCLE. No kyphosis/scoliosis.
Spine normal. Hip joint normal. Vitals.

INSPECTION
  Attitude — Hip: neutral. Knee: flexed. Ankle: equinus, plantar flexion.
  Subtalar joint: varus — inverted.
  Talo-navicular joint: adducted.
  Medial border: concave, deep crease present.
  Lateral border: convex, stretched, callosities on lateral malleolus.
  Lateral malleolus and head of talus: prominent.

PALPATION
  Not warm, no tenderness. Inspectory findings confirmed.
  Deformity — flexible or rigid.

MOVEMENTS
  Ankle: PF 40 degrees. DF restricted / not possible.
  Subtalar: inversion 30 degrees. Eversion restricted.
  Talonavicular: abduction —; adduction positive.
  Left leg: normal, no deformity. Adequate correction with 10 degrees DF.

MEASUREMENTS                  (R)         (L)
  Thigh                       17 cm       17 cm
  Leg                         15 cm       17 cm
  → 2 cm shortening of (R) leg segment.
  Distal neurovascular status normal. No lymphadenopathy.
  Other joints normal, other limb normal.

DIAGNOSIS: a case of partially corrected CTEV on (R) foot with shortening of leg.

INVESTIGATIONS
  X-ray AP view: tarso-metatarsal angle.
  X-ray lateral view: TALO-CALCANEAL (KITE) ANGLE — normal > 35 degrees;
  DECREASED in CTEV.

TREATMENT — correction then maintenance; operative and non-operative.

  Non-operative:
    * Manipulation alone (minor deformity) — dorsiflexion and eversion held
      5 seconds, repeated for 5 minutes, by the mother.
    * Manipulation + POP (major deformity):
        Kite's  → below-knee cast
        Ponseti → above-knee cast

  Operative:
    1. Postero-medial soft tissue release (PMSTR)
    2. Limited soft tissue release — equinus: posterior release;
       adduction: medial release; cavus: plantar release
    3. Tendon transfers (only after 5 years) → tibialis anterior tendon
    4. Dwyer's osteotomy (wedge osteotomy of calcaneum, ≥ 3 years)
    5. Dilwyn Evans procedure (PMSTR with calcaneo-cuboid fusion), 4–8 years
    6. Wedge tarsectomy (8–11 years; wedge removed from mid-tarsal area)
    7. Triple arthrodesis (> 12 years; fusion of subtalar, calcaneo-cuboid and
       talo-navicular joints)
    8. Ilizarov's technique (neglected cases; gradual stretching using external
       fixation) → JESS fixation

  Maintenance:
    1. CTEV splints (plastic, moulded, tied with straps)
    2. Dennis-Brown splint — until the child starts walking; used 24x7
    3. CTEV shoes (during walking, < 5 years):
         (i) straight inner border to prevent forefoot adduction
         (ii) outer shoe raise to prevent foot inversion
         (iii) no heel to prevent equinus

  JESS: differential distraction — medial 1 mm, lateral 0.5 mm distraction.
        1 to 4 years. Tibia. Educate parents. Pin size 4.5 mm.


2. CHRONIC OSTEOMYELITIS
========================
Chief complaint: discharge from upper part of front of leg x 2 years.

HOPI: apparently normal before 2 years, then sustained trauma to (R) leg for
which he was operated. After 1 month, noticed throbbing pain and discharge at
the site of operation.
  Discharge (oozing from the surgical site):
    - continuous, purulent, scanty, for 2 years
    - not foul smelling, not blood stained
    - H/o BONY SPICULES in the discharge
    - pain aggravated on walking, relieved by rest

Past H/o: known hypertensive. Not a known case of DM, TB, epilepsy, cardiac
disorders.
Personal H/o: mixed diet, bowel and bladder normal, alcohol and smoking.
Treatment H/o.

General examination: conscious, oriented, moderately built and nourished.
No PICCLE. Pedal oedema diffuse from foot to knee. Skin hyperpigmented and
thickened over front of leg. Smooth surface. No apparent muscle wasting or limb
shortening. Vitals: PR, RR, BP.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS: implant infection; tuberculous osteomyelitis; soft
tissue infection; Ewing's sarcoma.

INVESTIGATIONS
  1. X-ray — thickening; patchy sclerosis; SEQUESTRUM (dense area, decreased
     vascularity); INVOLUCRUM; CLOACA
  2. Sinogram
  3. CT, MRI
  4. Blood (ESR raised)
  5. Pus culture

TREATMENT
  1. Sequestrectomy
  2. Saucerisation
  3. Saline irrigation
  4. Skin graft
  5. Skeletal stabilisation

COMPLICATIONS
  * Acute exacerbation
  * Growth abnormalities (shortening, lengthening, deformity)
  * Pathological fracture
  * SINUS TRACT MALIGNANCY (squamous cell carcinoma)
  * Amyloidosis (rare)


3. NON-UNION
============
Chief complaints: deformity of (R) upper limb for 1 month; pain during movement
of limb for 1 month.

HOPI: apparently normal before 12 years, then sustained a fracture of the (R)
upper limb, treated elsewhere. Apparently normal until 1 month ago, then
noticed a deformity of the (R) upper limb associated with:
  Pain — pricking type, radiating towards (R) hand, aggravated by movements,
  relieved by rest.
  - H/o difficulty using the (R) arm for 1 month
  - H/o ABNORMAL MOBILITY
  - No H/o fever, open wound, discharge
  - NO H/O NATIVE TREATMENT / OIL MASSAGE   ← asked explicitly

Past H/o: not a known case of DM, HT, TB, epilepsy, cardiac disorders. No
previous surgery.
Personal H/o: mixed diet; not alcoholic, not a smoker.

General examination: conscious, oriented, moderately built and nourished, no
PICCLE, no deformities. Vitals: PR, RR, BP.
Examination of upper limb: after consent, exposed from the shoulder, sitting.

DIAGNOSIS: a case of post-traumatic non-union of (R) distal humerus with
shortening of (R) arm.

INVESTIGATIONS
  X-ray — OBLITERATED MEDULLARY CAVITY, smooth and sclerotic fracture lines.

TREATMENT
  1. Open reduction, internal fixation and BONE GRAFTING
  2. Excision of fragments (with replacement by artificial moulds)
  3. Ilizarov's external fixation (rare)
  4. If asymptomatic — no treatment

5. OSTEOARTHRITIS (KNEE)
========================
Chief complaints: pain and stiffness over (R) knee joint for 3 months; swelling
over (R) knee joint for 4 months.

HOPI: apparently normal before 4 months, after which developed:
  Swelling — gradual in onset, present continuously.
  Pain for 3 months — gradual in onset, progressive, unilateral, dull aching,
    continuous, AGGRAVATED BY MOVEMENTS, RELIEVED BY REST.
  Stiffness for 3 months — aggravated on sitting for a long duration,
    NO EARLY MORNING STIFFNESS, relieved after walking.
  H/o difficulty in squatting
  H/o difficulty in sitting cross-legged
  H/o difficulty in climbing stairs
  H/o inability to bear weight on the affected side
  H/o restricted movements
  H/o CREPITUS on movement

TREATMENT
  Medical:
    * Analgesics (diclofenac, COX-2 inhibitors, morphine)
    * Chondroprotective agents (chondroitin sulphate, glucosamine) — rarely used
    * Viscosupplements (rare)
    * WEIGHT REDUCTION
    * Avoid stress and strain to the joint
    * Local heat — pain relief
    * Exercises — QUADRICEPS STRENGTHENING
  Surgical:
    * High tibial osteotomy (mild cases, and for genu varum)
    * Total knee replacement (severe cases)


6. MALUNION
===========

HOPI: apparently normal for 2 months, then while walking accidentally fell down.
  - developed pain and swelling in the middle of the (R) leg, lasted 1 week,
    treated with analgesics, subsided in 4 days
  - before 20 days, while walking, her leg SUDDENLY BENT OUTWARD, with pain,
    not radiating, dull aching, aggravated on walking
  - H/o difficulty standing and walking on her own, but able to stand with
    support
  - H/o restriction of movements at the ankle joint
  - NO H/O NATIVE TREATMENT
  - NO H/O OIL MASSAGE
  - No H/o operative interventions
  - No H/o fever, discharge, open wounds

Past H/o: no similar illness. Not a known case of DM, TB, bronchial asthma,
epilepsy. No drug intake. No previous surgery.
Personal H/o: mixed diet; bowel and bladder normal; alcohol, smoking.
